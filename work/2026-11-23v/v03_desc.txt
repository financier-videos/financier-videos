Which bill do you hold onto longest? The $10 wears out first — about 5.7 years. A $1 lasts about 7.2 years, and a $100 about 24 years, because it mostly sits in drawers.
These are Federal Reserve estimates of average lifespan. U.S. bills are 75% cotton and 25% linen.

Sources:
• Federal Reserve Board, "How long is the lifespan of U.S. paper money?" (estimates; cited by St. Louis Fed and San Francisco Fed)
• Bureau of Engraving and Printing — currency paper composition
