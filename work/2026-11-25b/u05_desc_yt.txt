Ever checked the price a month before?
How a fake "50% off" tag can be built: raise the "was" price for a few weeks, then "cut" it back to where it started. The FTC's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR 233.1) say a former price should be the actual price the item was openly offered at, for a reasonably substantial period of time — a price inflated just to advertise a big reduction is called fictitious. Massachusetts spells it out: the former price must have been offered for at least 28 of the 90 days before the ad (940 CMR 6.05). WalletHub's 2025 Black Friday study compared 3,100 items with their Oct 27–Nov 17 prices: about 36% offered no savings and 9.5% cost more.
The $50 / $100 example is our illustration, not a specific store or product.
